# Handheld camera rig — live screen reading on the go

A clip-on camera for playing Champions in handheld mode while the app
tracks the battle. Hooks over the Switch's top edge, a thin boom arcs
forward over the screen, and a tiny Wi-Fi camera looks back-down at it.

**Why not Bluetooth / a GoPro?** Bluetooth can't carry video (~1–2 Mbps —
every "Bluetooth camera" streams over Wi-Fi and uses BT only for pairing).
Action cams fail on physics: a GoPro (~120 g) and the Insta360 GO 3S
(39 g) both have minimum focus distances of 0.3–0.5 m — at ~13 cm from
the screen everything is blur — and neither exposes a stream a
third-party app may read. So: a 5-gram dev-board camera we fully control.

## The printed part (`switch_cam_mount.stl`)

Generated by `switch_cam_mount.py` (edit parameters, re-run
`python3 switch_cam_mount.py` — needs `numpy-stl`). Validated geometry:

- Clamp gap 14.4 mm — fits **every Switch model** (all are 13.9 mm thick)
  with foam lining; front lip drops only 6 mm, staying on the bezel.
- Bridge has vent-relief cutouts and the whole clamp **slides along the
  edge** — position it clear of the vent slots and game-card slot.
- Boom: 96 mm at 30°, camera ends ~84 mm in front / ~51 mm above the
  screen plane → 128 mm to screen center, aim error 1.2°, incidence 49°
  from the screen normal. The rig sits above your sightline to the screen.
- Battery tray rides low on the back plate — counterweights the boom.
  Total added mass ≈ 30 g (14 g print + 5 g board + 10 g battery).

**Print:** PETG (or PLA+), 0.2 mm layers, 3 perimeters, 25–30 % infill,
back plate flat on the bed, tree supports under the boom and head.
No printer? Upload the STL to [Craftcloud](https://craftcloud3d.com),
pick PETG + supports, and compare vendor quotes (~$10–20 shipped).
The vault note's §11.1 has the full order table with links.

## Assembly

1. Line both clamp jaws with foam tape.
2. Swap the camera module for the 120° one (flip the socket latch gently).
3. Solder the LiPo to the XIAO's BAT pads (switch inline if you want one).
4. Slide the board into the head tray **lens-down over the window**,
   zip-tie through the bars under the plate. Battery into the back tray.
5. Flash the webcam firmware (Seeed wiki "XIAO ESP32S3 Sense — camera
   web server"; the stock example works). Point it at your **phone's
   hotspot**; the stream is `http://<board-ip>:81/stream` (MJPEG).
6. Clip on, open the stream in a browser, and **unscrew the lens ~¼ turn**
   until screen text at 13 cm is sharp. Done — it holds focus forever.

## App integration status

The app's recognition engines already accept any JPEG frame; a "network
camera" source in the capture screen (pull frames from the MJPEG URL
instead of the phone camera) is the remaining software step — small,
planned next. Until then the rig streams to any browser/IP-cam viewer.

**Cheap validation before printing:** hold your phone where the camera
will sit (~13 cm out, ~50° down-angle, centered) and run a preview scan
on that photo. That's exactly what the rig will see — if recognition is
happy, print with confidence. The exemplar library will re-learn from
the rig's own viewpoint quickly either way.
